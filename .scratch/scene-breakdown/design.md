# Scene Breakdown design

## Status and purpose

Implemented design. [Scene Breakdown and Recap](../../specs/scene-breakdown.md) defines the resolved production contract; [Ledger](../../specs/ledger.md) defines the paired v4 record. This document retains the product rationale.

Scene Breakdown is a compact, structured account of every scene in a Session. It serves two uses:

- Generate the existing short, human-readable Recap Summary without processing the full Ledger again.
- Give an LLM enough compact history to read a whole Campaign, with references back to detailed Ledger evidence when needed.

The artifact is named **Scene Breakdown**, its Pydantic model is **`SceneBreakdown`**, and its filename is **`scene_breakdown.json`**. It is registered in the application's artifact list but hidden from the UI's artifact display. It has no deterministic Markdown companion. The existing `recap_summary.md` remains the human-facing recap.

## Joint generation and shared context

Generate the Ledger and Scene Breakdown in the same LLM call from the same permitted Role Transcript material. Do not generate the breakdown by summarizing an already-condensed Ledger: that would lose original phrasing and distinctive details that the Ledger intentionally omits.

The structured generation response has three top-level components:

```text
starting_situation
ledger
scene_breakdown
```

`starting_situation` appears once in this response. Neither nested payload duplicates it. Application code injects this same value into both persisted artifacts. The generation payloads and persisted models therefore have different shapes; application-owned metadata need not be generated by the LLM.

The breakdown also contains its own `ending_situation`. This describes established state at the end of play and explicitly stated next moves, distinguishing plans, desires, and demands from accepted commitments or completed actions. It should account for separated characters where relevant; the last scene played may not describe the whole party's position.

Each Session is generated independently. Do not supply previous Sessions' Ledgers or breakdowns as additional narrative context. Campaign-wide connections can be made later by a consumer reading the accumulated breakdowns.

### Source rules

Preserve the boundaries defined by [Transcript Sections](../../specs/transcript-sections.md):

- The shared starting situation uses the designated starting-context material.
- Regular Ledger entries and scene content use permitted current-session material. Opening recap and character introductions do not become new scenes or current-session events.
- Scene events, outcomes, and carry-forward facts must agree with the generated Ledger, including accepted corrections and unresolved uncertainty.
- Titles, location phrasing, and signature details may draw directly from permitted current-session transcript material, even when their wording or descriptive color is omitted from the Ledger.
- Use phrases spoken in the Session for titles and locations when appropriate to recognition. Do not invent quotations, atmosphere, motives, humor, or thematic symbolism.
- Keep the existing glossary and attribution constraints; glossary information does not authorize introducing absent facts.

Joint generation preserves access to original evidence, but does not guarantee agreement or quality. Whether the combined task harms Ledger quality remains an empirical quality question; structural validation alone does not establish semantic quality.

## Scene contents

JSON is the persisted structured format, validated with Pydantic. The agreed semantic contents are:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | A short, recognizable scene title; reuse appropriate Session phrasing. |
| `location` | One source-supported location name or phrase, or null if unknown. No separate canonical-name and remembered-phrase fields. |
| `participants` | Characters and groups central to the scene. |
| `situation` | Circumstances or problem at the scene's start, incorporating a goal when established. No separate goal field. |
| `outcome` | What happened, including failure, interruption, or an unresolved ending. |
| `carry_forward` | A list of concise strings preserving additional discoveries, commitments, relationships, possessions, threats, and other facts useful for continuity. |
| `signature_detail` | A required, nullable field for one distinctive sensory, thematic, or personal detail. |
| `ledger_ranges` | Nonempty list of inclusive zero-based `{start_index, end_index}` references; multiple noncontiguous ranges support interleaving. |

At Session level, the strict persisted v1 schema contains session_id, session_name, starting_situation, ledger_sha256, ending_situation and ordered scenes. Application supplies identity and the generation-time Ledger digest. Text is trimmed/nonempty; participants and carry_forward are string lists; an empty Ledger has an empty scene list.

### Compression

Represent **every scene**, not just scenes currently judged consequential. Downstream recap generation selects what deserves inclusion in a short recap. Preserving all scenes avoids predicting which encounters will matter later in a Campaign.

Use concise fields with detail proportional to substance rather than a fixed word limit. Aim for roughly one sentence each for situation, outcome, and signature detail, plus short carry-forward items. Dense scenes can take more space. Do not reproduce every Ledger fact.

`outcome` records what happened; `carry_forward` retains additional useful facts without repeating the outcome. There are no separate discovery and lasting-change fields, and no typed categories for carry-forward items. Attribution and uncertainty are expressed in the strings themselves.

### Signature detail and recognizable wording

Prefer the detail a player would use to remind another player which scene this was. It can be sensory, thematic, or personal; it need not cover all three. Some overlap with outcome is acceptable because this field identifies what should survive further compression.

Use the most recognizable supported detail available, even if ordinary. Allow `null` when no useful detail exists rather than manufacturing distinctiveness. The field itself remains required.

For example, in Brandonsford Session 002, Trout accidentally hitting Squints with his sling during the rescue is a strong signature detail. George's account of amputating his poisoned arm gives his dragon warning a personal identity. These are historical examples from Brandonsford Session 002; they are explanatory, not bundled fixtures or prerequisites. Do not invent an absent detail merely to fill a signature field.

### Time pressure

Record new, advancing, or resolved clocks and threats in `carry_forward` as ordinary strings. There is no separate clock schema. Preserve the actual specificity of the evidence: “George warns the dragon will soon fly; no deadline is specified” must not become an invented countdown.

Do not infer elapsed time from a retracted event. In the Brandonsford faun-wine example, Dunk's initial five-hour unconsciousness result was corrected; neither the unconsciousness nor the lost time should survive into the breakdown.

## Scene boundaries, ordering, and references

A scene is a coherent interaction or problem. Location changes can suggest boundaries, but a new problem at the same location can be a new scene.

Combine continuing interactions across table cutaways. Dunk's interrupted conversation with Ingrid is one scene even when play cuts to the others catching Naggeneen. A later visit or new problem can begin another scene. Order interleaved scenes by their first appearance in the Ledger; do not reconstruct a more elaborate simultaneous timeline.

Every Ledger entry belongs to **exactly one scene**. Ranges partition the Ledger completely:

- Endpoints must identify existing Ledger entries and ranges must be well formed.
- No entry may be assigned more than once, within one scene or across scenes.
- No entry may be left unassigned.
- One scene may own several noncontiguous ranges.

Assignment does not require the scene text to repeat every assigned fact. It provides a coverage check and a retrieval path, not a claim that all details survived compression.

References point only to Ledger entries. Do not add transcript references for signature details or reused wording. Consequently, a Ledger range may support the scene's events without containing every descriptive phrase in its breakdown. Those details are grounded through the generation source rules.

## Persistence, dependencies, and UI behavior

Treat the Ledger and Scene Breakdown as a matched pair:

- Regenerate both together; do not offer independent regeneration of either artifact.
- Validate both payloads and their cross-artifact coverage before saving either.
- Accept or reject a generation candidate as a pair; never combine artifacts from different attempts.
- Build both persisted artifacts with the same generated starting situation.
- Preserve the pair's consistency when replacing files. Stage Ledger JSON, Ledger Markdown and Scene Breakdown as one rollback unit. Handled failures restore previous bytes; interruption or failed rollback leaves `.ledger-generation-incomplete` to block readiness until regeneration.

The Ledger retains its existing deterministic `ledger.md` companion. Scene Breakdown has no equivalent companion.

The dependency change is:

```text
Role Transcript + Transcript Sections
    -> joint generation
        -> Ledger -> detailed Summary
        -> Scene Breakdown -> existing Recap Summary
```

The recap's narrative source is Scene Breakdown rather than Ledger. The detailed Summary continues to use the Ledger. Existing non-narrative inputs such as Session metadata are not redesigned here.

Scene Breakdown is registered in `ArtifactName` and `ARTIFACTS` in [paths.py](../../packages/tablesage-application/src/tablesage_application/paths.py), with filename `scene_breakdown.json`, display name `Scene Breakdown`, `should_show_in_ui=False`, and no companion filenames. Its registry category is `FROM_TRANSCRIPT`; the dependency graph, not category-based deletion, controls freshness. Artifact existence remains available to code even though the artifact is hidden from the UI display.

Upstream changes make the shared producer stale and regenerate both siblings. Editing one sibling affects only its actual consumers; ranges and digest are generation-time provenance and are not revalidated against later Ledger edits. A Session with Ledger but no breakdown has an incomplete producer and must regenerate it before using the breakdown.

## Resolved mechanics and remaining quality work

The response contains exactly starting_situation, ledger and scene_breakdown; there is no generation scratchpad. Ranges are canonicalized by first Ledger index and must partition the Ledger exactly once. Candidates are accepted/rejected as pairs with bounded retries. Recap uses Scene Breakdown, validates flat bullets, adds the Recap heading and persists atomically; production does not reject on its prompt's word budget.

For implementation details see [scene_breakdown.py](../../packages/tablesage-application/src/tablesage_application/session_pipeline/scene_breakdown.py), [Ledger generation](../../.documentation/generate_ledger.md), [recap generation](../../packages/tablesage-application/src/tablesage_application/session_pipeline/generate_recap_summary.py) and [dependency tracking](../artifact-dependency-tracking/design.md).

Full-session semantic quality evaluation remains distinct from schema validation. Review should examine interleaved interactions, corrected facts, recognizable source phrasing, compactness and recap usefulness. The illustrative scenes above do not constitute that evaluation.
