# Experiment 7 — richer speaker-assignment decision rule

Status: Tried. Result: **Success, small and not yet adopted** — duration contains useful decision
signal beyond cosine-similarity margin, but the absolute-best-similarity gate is less reliable than
the preliminary replay suggested. A conservative duration-conditioned rule improves both frozen
sessions and reduces the pooled wrong-label count by one; the unconstrained pooled winner improves
the headline score further by accepting a materially higher error and misattributed-audio cost.

## Motivation

Production's matcher assigns the closest speaker only when the cosine-similarity gap between the
best and second-best centroids is at least `0.08`. Margin is a useful ambiguity measure, but it
discards two signals already available at decision time:

- **Absolute best similarity.** `0.75 vs. 0.70` and `0.15 vs. 0.10` have the same margin even
  though the former resembles a known speaker much more strongly.
- **Utterance duration.** Longer clips contain more speech frames and generally produce more
  reliable embeddings than sub-second clips.

The hypothesis was that these signals could recover some of production's 80 unassigned
utterances without paying for too many new wrong assignments.

## Implementation

`MarginAndSimilarityMatcher` adds two independent relaxations to the baseline rule:

1. an optional absolute-best-similarity OR-gate;
2. duration-specific margin thresholds, with the most specific configured duration cutoff taking
   precedence.

The harness now passes each utterance's duration to every matcher; the baseline ignores this
optional metadata. NaN similarity results remain unassigned regardless of any relaxation.

The experiment driver prepares the two sessions' embeddings and reference centroids once, then
scores every matcher configuration against the same prepared evidence. That avoids rebuilding
identical centroids inside the parameter loop and makes the decision-rule sweep independent of
model inference.

Unit tests pin baseline compatibility, absolute-similarity assignment, duration-boundary
behavior, and NaN handling.

## Sweep

Using production's `wespeaker-resnet34` embedder and the same 438 scored utterances as prior
experiments, the grid contains **840** combinations:

| Parameter | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Base margin | 0.06, 0.07, 0.08, 0.09, 0.10 |
| Absolute-best threshold | off, 0.30, 0.325, 0.35, 0.375, 0.40, 0.425, 0.45 |
| Duration cutoff | off, 1.0s, 1.5s, 2.0s, 2.5s, 3.0s |
| Margin at/above duration cutoff | 0.00, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06 |

Full results are in
[`experiment-7-grid-results.csv`](experiment-7-grid-results.csv), generated by
[`run_experiment_7.py`](run_experiment_7.py).

## Results

### Pooled maximum versus robust selection

| Candidate | Score | Accuracy | Unassigned | Error | Wrong count | Misattributed audio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Production: margin ≥0.08 | 0.904 | 79.5% | 18.3% | 2.3% | 10 | 9.0s |
| Pooled maximum: margin ≥0.10 OR best ≥0.375; ≥1s margin ≥0.00 | **0.911** | **82.2%** | **14.8%** | 3.0% | 13 | 17.4s |
| Robust winner: margin ≥0.10; ≥1s margin ≥0.04 | 0.908 | 80.1% | 17.8% | **2.1%** | **9** | 10.0s |

The pooled maximum converts 15 net abstentions into 12 correct and 3 wrong assignments. Under the
benchmark's cost model that is a positive trade, but the secondary harm is substantial: three
additional errors and 8.5 additional seconds attributed to the wrong speaker. Four absolute-best
thresholds from 0.375 through 0.45 produce exactly the same output at that point, so the maximum is
not sensitive to the precise absolute threshold within that range.

The robust rule was selected by maximizing the **worst per-session score improvement**, rather
than pooled score alone, while checking error-rate behavior. It is a piecewise margin rule:

```text
duration < 1 second:  assign when margin >= 0.10
duration >= 1 second: assign when margin >= 0.04
```

It changes 16 of 438 decisions. Relative to production, the final pooled counts are three more
correct, two fewer unassigned, and one fewer wrong. Misattributed time still rises by 1.05 seconds
despite the lower wrong count because the remaining/new errors are slightly longer.

### Per-session robustness

| Session | Candidate | Score | Accuracy | Unassigned | Error | Wrong count |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `20260818-end` | Production | 0.913 | 82.1% | 15.4% | 2.6% | 5 |
|  | Robust rule | **0.917** | **83.1%** | **14.4%** | 2.6% | 5 |
| `20260825-end` | Production | 0.897 | 77.4% | 20.6% | 2.1% | 5 |
|  | Robust rule | **0.901** | **77.8%** | 20.6% | **1.6%** | **4** |

Both sessions improve by almost the same amount (+0.0041). On the first session the rule trades
among correct, wrong, and unassigned labels while holding five total errors; on the second it
removes one error without changing the unassigned count.

As a two-session leave-one-session-out check, selecting the best configuration on
`20260818-end` chooses this exact robust rule; it still improves the held-out `20260825-end`
score from 0.897 to 0.901. The reverse direction is not stable: selecting solely on
`20260825-end` chooses an aggressive zero-margin duration override that scores well there but
raises the first session's error rate to 5.1%. This asymmetry is the strongest reason not to adopt
the pooled maximum.

### Feature ablation

Best pooled point in each rule family:

| Family | Score | Accuracy | Unassigned | Error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Margin only | 0.904 | 79.5% | 18.3% | 2.3% |
| Absolute-best gate, no duration | 0.909 | 82.0% | 14.8% | 3.2% |
| Duration override, no absolute gate | 0.910 | 82.0% | 15.1% | 3.0% |
| Combined | **0.911** | **82.2%** | **14.8%** | 3.0% |

Duration supplies most of the pooled gain. Adding the absolute-best gate to the best duration-only
point recovers only one additional correct utterance. More importantly, every aggressive family
winner raises the error rate. The clean robust result uses duration but deliberately omits the
absolute gate.

## Interpretation

The central hypothesis is partly confirmed: a single global margin threshold is leaving useful
duration information on the floor. Short clips benefit from a stricter bar, while clips of at
least one second can tolerate a lower one. This also explains why the robust base margin rises
from 0.08 to 0.10—the rule is not merely relaxing assignment; it becomes more conservative on the
least reliable clips at the same time.

The absolute-best-similarity hypothesis is weaker. High cosine similarity does not ensure the
winning speaker is uniquely plausible, especially when multiple centroids are close. On these
fixtures it mostly lowers abstention, with an error cost large enough that duration alone is the
safer feature.

The gain remains small and highly selected: two frozen sessions, 438 utterances, and only 16
changed decisions for the robust rule. The grid was tuned on these same sessions, so even the
leave-one-session-out check is weak evidence, not a substitute for new data.

## Decision and next steps

- Keep the robust rule registered in the benchmark as `margin-duration-robust` so future sessions
  can test it without repeating the implementation.
- Do **not** port it to `tablesage-tools.speakers.identify_speakers` or add production settings yet.
- Re-run the fixed rule—without retuning—after adding more hand-corrected sessions. Adoption should
  require the per-session improvement and non-increasing error count to hold out of sample.
- Treat the absolute-best OR-gate as not supported for production by this experiment. If revisited,
  prefer a calibrated/normalized score or a learned rule evaluated on separate validation data.
