# The talking frog

**Attendees:** erik saltwell (Sir Phidipaldi) · jason beaumont (Game Master) · john schork (Waymon Tisdale) · marshall rise (Dunk) · rich gredzinski (Dr Uriah)

## Recap

1. In a tavern in another city, the party played in a five-person card game; the fifth player was caught cheating and lost, leaving a four-way tie.
2. The four jointly won a treasure map marking several locations around a town called Brandonsford, though they don't know what the marked locations are; they assume the treasure is at one of them.
3. Each came to the game for their own reasons; Dr Uriah gambles to fund his scholarly work and buy dark secrets.
4. The party followed the map through unknown land to Brandonsford.

**Opening:** The party approaches Brandonsford at nightfall and is hailed by a member of the town's night watch.

## Characters

- **Waymon Tisdale** — Waymon Tisdale, a Dagonite assassin.
- **Dunk** — Dunk, a Trollkin wanderer who prefers to be alone in the wilderness.
- **Sir Phidipaldi** — Sir Phidipaldi, a human errant fool. He carries a huge lance named Lady Isaboob and a two-handed sword named Sir Chumunga, both clearly too big for him, and wears plate mail he cannot properly use (it counts only as chain mail).
- **Dr Uriah** — Dr Uriah Marsh, a Dagonite metaphysician and scholar. Dagonites look human but are extremely pale with unusual eyes.

## Session

1. Brandonsford is a quiet little town on the edge of the wilderness: thatched-roof stone cottages, two big plaster-and-timber inns facing each other — the Clumsy Fox Tavern and the Golden Egg Tavern — and an ancient small stone wall around the town. There are no guards or proper law enforcement; the town is protected by a rotating group of able-bodied men on night watch.

2. **Night watchman:** Halt — who goes there, and what business do you have in this town?

3. **Sir Phidipaldi:** We are ancient travelers who claim the sacred law of Zeus for hospitality.

4. **Night watchman:** You're suspicious, and we don't get many visitors, but night is falling — better come in. There are two inns: the Clumsy Fox, which is busy, warm and cozy, and the Golden Egg across the street, which has far fewer people.

5. Down the street are a general store, a smith, a small church, and an alchemist shop; there is also a farm on the edge of town.

6. **Dr Uriah** — Before going into town, goes to the river and casts speak with aquatic animals, seeking an aquatic creature with an opinion on the town's inns. He meets a frog.

7. **Reginald:** The frog doesn't know which tavern is better, but warns that at night a little creature called a Clurichen comes from the river banks into town. He's a trickster and up to no good — wherever he goes, you want to stay away from.

8. The frog is named Reginald, and Dr Uriah has befriended him. *— rich gredzinski*

9. **Sir Phidipaldi:** Recommends the party go to the busier inn — likely the higher-status one, and better for gathering rumors.

10. **Dunk** — Splits off from the party to go scavenge at the farm on the edge of town, preferring to avoid the crowded tavern.

11. Waymon, Sir Phidipaldi, and Dr Uriah enter the Clumsy Fox Tavern: warm, fire going, and hopping. Behind the bar is Bentley, a jovial, talkative bald halfling with glasses; his wife carries food out from the kitchen and his three daughters work as servers. Among the patrons are a handful of adventurers: a red-headed noblewoman, a rail-thin magician, a greasy-looking thief, and a shifty-eyed little halfling.

12. **Sir Phidipaldi** — Approaches the red-headed noblewoman, greets her flamboyantly, and shows off his oversized lance, Lady Isaboob.

13. **Lady Hilda** — Receives Sir Phidipaldi with patient, unflappable gentleness and buys him a drink.

14. **Lady Hilda:** There are rumors of a dragon around the town. You should talk to Eric the Reeve — she points to a portly, mutton-chopped fellow across the tavern — he's been trying to hire somebody to kill it. She can't do it herself, but if you have a party of adventurers, she would join you for some money.

15. Lady Hilda wears chain mail and carries a shield; she looks more seasoned than the party members.

16. **Sir Phidipaldi:** We will go talk with the Reeve, then come back and consider availing ourselves of your services.

17. **Dr Uriah** — Scans the tavern's patrons for curses; he senses none, but notices the greasy-haired thief wears a little jar on a necklace with a wasp buzzing inside it. To learn the item's history with metaphysics he would need to get closer, so he resolves to try.

18. **Waymon Tisdale** — Chats with Bentley, the halfling bar owner.

19. **Bentley:** Introduces himself, pours a free first glass of his famous Fox Tail brew — which tastes fantastic — and offers rooms at 10 silver pieces a night. He's lived here his entire life, but things have gotten downhearted: there's the dragon rumor, and his restocking shipment is a week overdue — a problem for the whole town. Nothing like this has happened since Sir Brandon killed the first dragon hundreds of years ago. The townsfolk are simple and can't afford the adventurers here; the Reeve has been trying to get people to help.

20. The scene shifts to the farm on the edge of town, where Dunk approaches as the sun sets. Farmer Gill sits on his porch chewing straw and packing a pipe.

21. **Farmer Gill:** Remarks that Dunk would be amazing at farm work, and offers to sell him his mule for 100 gold so he could start his own farm.

22. Farmer Gill's mule — a shabby, low-rent creature — is named Seamus. *— rich gredzinski*

23. **Farmer Gill:** The big work needed is killing the dragon outside town. Long ago, Sir Brandon lived right around this town; he and his friends ventured into the woods and killed the dragon that had been terrorizing the town. A fairy in the woods gave Brandon a magic sword, and that sword killed the dragon. He was knighted and the town was named Brandonsford after him, but he didn't stay, and he has since died. That dragon was truly dead — they dragged it through town and ripped it apart hundreds of years ago — so this must be a different dragon. Gill himself saw the dragon; it saw him and wanted nothing to do with him, and he was scared. He sent his boys off after it — they're out trying to kill it right now, and he's sure they'll manage.

24. **? rich gredzinski:** How many sons did Farmer Gill send after the dragon? **→ jason beaumont:** Three — Gill insists they'll be fine.

25. **Dunk:** He's done a little farm work in his time, and doesn't really know what he is — when he was just a little dude, he was sent out of his village.

26. **Dunk** — Asks to sleep in Gill's barn tonight while he thinks things over; Gill agrees and fetches drinks from his still so the two can sit out on the porch.

27. Back at the Clumsy Fox, Sir Phidipaldi has finished talking to Lady Hilda and approaches Eric the Reeve, a portly man with big mutton chops.

28. **Eric the Reeve** — *Has purple rings around his eyes and looks visibly stressed.*

29. **Eric the Reeve:** He has a lot of problems — first of all, the dragon. He will pay 1000 gold to whoever kills the dragon and drags its body into town. A woodsman named George, who lives a couple houses down and might show up tonight, encountered the dragon and lost his arm to it. George says it's a black dragon, not a big one. George is capable but one-armed, so he can't kill it himself.

30. **Sir Phidipaldi:** Committing a faux pas, he lectures Eric that there is no such thing as black dragons — that verified sightings are actually illusions created by Clurichens, small river creatures that trick people and sometimes steal their limbs.

31. **Dr Uriah** — *Checking his lore, knows Sir Phidipaldi has it wrong — and that Eric the Reeve knows it too.*

32. **Eric the Reeve** — *Looks offended.*

33. **Eric the Reeve:** He's known George all his life — there's no way he lied. How would a Clurichen take his arm? George doesn't even know where his arm is.

34. **Sir Phidipaldi** — Takes off his glove, slaps Eric the Reeve across the face with it, and challenges him to a duel at dawn.

35. **Eric the Reeve** — *Visibly flustered and at a loss for words, stammering that Sir Phidipaldi is obviously more skilled in adventure than he is.*

36. **Dr Uriah** — Buys two Fox Tail brews, sits down next to the greasy-haired, rotten-toothed, shifty-looking thief, and hands him one.

37. **Drop Dead Ned:** Introduces himself as Drop Dead Ned and calls the town a dump. The jar on his necklace holds a trained killer wasp — you let it out, it kills people, and it comes back. He and the wasp are for hire: 100 gold for the week plus 10% of the treasure, and he'll do anything as long as he's allowed to kill people with the wasp.

38. **Drop Dead Ned:** As Uriah examines the jar, Ned explains the wasp is for things he wants to kill fast — he prefers to kill things slow and watch them die. His counter-offer: 100 gold for the week, and every time Uriah lets him kill somebody his own way — just cutting them up, normal stuff — he'll give Uriah another five gold.

39. **Dr Uriah:** Introduces himself as Dr Uriah Marsh, a scholar looking for work, and says he'll think Ned's offer over.

40. The scene shifts back to Gill's farm, where Dunk and Farmer Gill sit on the porch getting loaded. Gill has told Dunk all about his sons, about Eric the town Reeve, and has started gossiping about the town.

41. **Farmer Gill:** The smith, Warwick, barely has anything for sale since nothing has come into town in months, and he keeps filling his shop with lucky charms — a rabbit's foot, a dream catcher, horseshoes, an entire wall of clovers — adding new ones every day and getting more and more paranoid. Most of what he sells is crap, but he has a beautiful Mithril chain shirt on display — probably the best chain armor in the world — that he always refuses to sell. Gill's theory: Ingrid, who sells the potions, has the hots for Warwick, and maybe he's putting up all the charms to keep her away.

42. **Farmer Gill** — *Starting to wobble from drink, but having the time of his life talking to Dunk.*

43. Back at the Clumsy Fox, the altercation between Eric the Reeve and Sir Phidipaldi grows more and more heated.

44. **Bentley:** Putting a hand on Sir Phidipaldi's shoulder: he wants no fighting in here. Phidipaldi's friends can stay, but since he caused trouble, he'll have to spend the night across the street at the Golden Egg. His drink is on the house.

45. **Bentley** — Escorts Sir Phidipaldi out of the Clumsy Fox, plate mail clanking, as the knight blusters about how Zeus's law of courtesy is no longer honored and doom is foretold for the tavern.

46. The Golden Egg Tavern is the opposite of the Clumsy Fox: nobody there, no music, no food — just Quinn, the white-bearded, bony, odd-looking owner tending bar, who is almost startled that anyone came in.

47. **Quinn:** Introduces himself as Quinn, the owner, and immediately charges 10 silver pieces for an ale — three times the price across the street — which turns out to be completely watered down. He apologizes: no matter how much good stuff he gets in, it's gone the next morning. He'll go to bed with a fully stocked bar, and by morning half the glasses are empty or knocked over. He believes Bentley steals it at night — that's why the Fox Tail brew is so popular.

48. **Quinn:** Offers 100 gold if Sir Phidipaldi helps him get back at Bentley. Since no real shipments have arrived in a while, his plan: stage a fake shipment — fill bottles with water, hide some downstairs, and make a big show of delivering ale — then watch to see what's really going on.

49. **Sir Phidipaldi:** Delighted to find courtesy is not lost in this town, he declares his shared hatred for the tavern across the street and agrees to the plan: Quinn should bring the bottles up, they'll fill them with water, and Phidipaldi will make a big show of coming in and delivering the ale.
