# Cross-Domain Research for the Scene Log Rubric

This records a borrowing-brilliance exploration conducted on 2026-10-06 to inform the quality rubric for [Improve the summary scene log for reincorporation](../item.md). It looked at how other fields judge great, very compact records of events. The resulting proposal is [rubric.md](../rubric.md); the full exchange, including the user's exact replies, is in [conversation-transcript.md](conversation-transcript.md).

Sourced observations are separated from the agent's interpretation. Each kernel is an exploratory possibility, not a validated design: evidence that a mechanism works in its source domain does not show that the adaptation works for Session summaries.

## Starting Evidence

The parent investigation ([Review session summary quality across various prompts](../../session-summary-prompt-quality/)) found that none of six prompt simplifications brought the Summary body near its 700-word target. Scene Breakdown remained most of the length: 743 words across 33 bullets in the baseline for Session **20260901**, including detail with no likely future use (the goblins' four names, "Squints pocketed some ooze"). Experiment details are in [experiments.md](../../session-summary-prompt-quality/experiments.md).

## Frame

**Agreed with the user:** Players returning to a Campaign need a Scene Breakdown they can absorb in a minute or two that brings back the scenes likely to matter again. They read it in the Summary before the next Session. The obstacle is that producing a complete, accurate retelling is easy, while judging in advance what will be reincorporated is hard. Constraints:

- All facts come from the Ledger, and nothing the players don't know may appear.
- The log is generated by an LLM with no knowledge of future Sessions.
- It is currently chronological and organized by scene.
- It sits beside Key Decisions & Events, Open Loops and Clocks, which already carry the forward-looking items.

**The scene log's job (user chose option A):** a **memory anchor**. It exists to give each forward-looking item the context a returning Player needs to remember why it matters; content nothing points back to is cut. The rejected alternative (B) was a standalone story recap that someone who missed the Session could follow.

**Working abstraction (agreed):** *Write the smallest set of memory cues that lets people who lived through events re-anchor each live thread.* It combines two candidate abstractions: support for a known set of forward claims, and cueing memory rather than retelling.

**Abstractions held in reserve:**

- **Handing over state to a successor:** turn a stream of events into the smallest state someone needs to carry on.
- **Choosing what to keep without knowing the future:** worth reopening if the forward sections themselves miss what later matters.

## Domain Findings

### 1. TV "Previously On…" Recaps (Near)

**Sources:**

- A recap brings viewers up to speed "on the parts of the arc relevant to the episode in question". Inclusion signals importance: "if you see something in 'Previously On' that didn't seem important previously, it will become important" ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main.PreviouslyOn); seen only through a search summary, because the page returned HTTP 403 when fetched).
- Derek Johnson describes recaps as "reordering old scenes within new sequences, relationships, and meanings". He says they reveal "what the producers think the episode is about", and names the craft tension: using them skillfully "without them becoming an expositional crutch" ([MediaCommons, *In Media Res*](https://mediacommons.org/imr/node/285)).
- Recaps often compress an episode into about 30 seconds of clips ([UCLan practice brief, via search summary](https://www5.uclan.ac.uk/sites/ImageBank/Marketing_Image_Library/Media_and_TV_Practice_Project_(campaign_style)_proofread.pdf)).
- **Limits:** no primary interview with an editor describing the selection process was found. Fetches of [alex4d](https://blog.alex4d.com/2008/10/01/previously-on-editing-organazized/) (speculative, no selection criteria) and [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2009/05/13/104090365/previously-on-for-tv-catch-ups-a-golden-age) (timed out) yielded nothing usable. The claim that recaps are built backward from the coming episode is an interpretation of the sources.

**Mechanism (interpretation):** selection is driven backward from a known payoff. Each clip is one recognizable moment. Because inclusion implies importance, irrelevant clips actively mislead.

**Transfer:** the upcoming episode corresponds to the forward sections; a clip to a bullet that brings back one moment; viewers who watched to players who were present. Trivia is a false setup, not neutral filler. **Mismatch:** a TV editor knows the next episode, while the scene log only has the same LLM's forward sections as a stand-in for the future.

**Kernel: recap-grade scene log.** Three proposed dimensions:

- **Payoff anchoring:** each bullet sets up a forward item, and each forward item has its setup.
- **Signal honesty:** no false setups.
- **Cue economy:** one recognizable moment per bullet.

**Open assumption:** GMs reincorporate things nobody flagged, so strict anchoring could cut them.

**User reaction:** asked whether the rubric should be strict (every bullet must anchor a forward item) or allow some untraced texture, the user replied "yes this seems good". The agent read this as favoring strictness and flagged the interpretation. The case-brief domain then offered a middle position.

### 2. Legal Case Briefs and Statements of Facts (Adjacent)

**Sources:**

- A fact belongs in a case brief only if it is "legally relevant or material, that is, if a change in that fact would have changed the way the law was applied". Irrelevant details make a brief "less brief and may distract you from the points that actually matter" ([MSU Law, *Briefing a Case*](https://www.law.msu.edu/rwa/briefing_a_case.pdf), via search summary because the PDF returned HTTP 404 when fetched; also the [ABA guide to case briefing](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_students/resources/student-lawyer/student-essentials/guide-to-case-briefing-in-law-school/), via search summary).
- CUNY Law's checklist for a court brief's Statement of Facts ([CUNY Legal Writing Center](https://www.law.cuny.edu/academics/academic-resources-support/legal-writing-center/student-resources/drafting-briefs-to-a-court/), fetched):
  - Include "all legally significant facts", "relevant background facts" and "facts that have an emotional resonance".
  - Ask: "Does the Statement include the facts that you use in the Argument?"
  - Edit to "remove legal conclusions and editorializing".
  - Present "a narrative that is easy to follow for a reader who is unfamiliar with the case".

**Mechanism:**

1. A counterfactual test for materiality.
2. Tiered facts: load-bearing facts always; background only in their service; emotional facts when they explain motive.
3. Two-way coverage between facts and argument.
4. Separating facts from conclusions.

**Transfer:** the holding and the argument correspond to the forward sections; a legally significant fact to an anchor bullet; a background fact to minimal orientation, often carried by the scene title; an emotional fact to a relationship or motive beat.

**Kernel: material-fact scene log.**

- The **deletion test** becomes the scoring operation: remove the bullet; does any forward item lose its setup?
- Background is allowed only in service of an anchor, which gives a principled middle ground on strictness.
- New dimension: **fact–significance separation.** The log never says why something matters, and the forward sections never retell. This would also remove the duplication between Key Decisions and Scene Breakdown seen in the experiments.

**Scenario (baseline bullets):**

- "Squints pocketed some ooze" is cut.
- Ned's 5-gold offer for the goblin bodies, against the goblins' wish to bury them, is kept because it anchors the unburied-dead clock.
- The mosaics' real gemstones are cut by a strict test.

**Open assumption:** the gemstones are where the analogy breaks. Law's holding is fixed and known; ours is incomplete. A latent resource that no forward item names is exactly the "new utility" that the project's reincorporation guidance asks players to discover, and a strict deletion test would systematically remove such resources.

**User reaction:** "great this sounds good". The latent-resource question was not answered and remains open.

### 3. Medical Shift Handoff, I-PASS (Adjacent)

**Sources:**

- I-PASS stands for Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness and contingency planning, and Synthesis by receiver ([Johns Hopkins Medicine](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/articles/reducing-medical-errors-in-patient-handoffs); [AHRQ TeamSTEPPS](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/ipass.html)).
- The 2014 NEJM study reported roughly a 30% drop in injuries from medical errors across the participating sites ([Johns Hopkins](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/articles/reducing-medical-errors-in-patient-handoffs); [MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/handoff-tool-cuts-harmful-medical-errors-30-percent/)).
- Patients are labeled stable, watcher ("unsure of illness trajectory") or unstable/star. In one study, watcher patients had an odds ratio of 2.6, and star patients 5.2, for an overnight rapid-response call compared with stable patients ([PMC7594794](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7594794), via search summary).
- An emergency-department adaptation found: "we don't need the full hospital course, just a brief synopsis". Clinicians valued a "pointed action plan rather than nebulous recommendations" ([WestJEM, PMC5102604](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102604), fetched).
- Synthesis by receiver: the person taking over restates the handoff, asks questions and confirms the key actions ([Vanderbilt handover guide, via search summary](https://vimbook.vumc.org/hospital-medicine/high-quality-handovers)).

**Mechanism:**

1. Triage first decides how much detail each item gets.
2. History is compressed to whatever explains the current state.
3. Understanding is checked at the receiver's end, not completeness at the sender's.

**Transfer:** a patient corresponds to a live thread; stable, watcher and unstable to resolved, uncertain and imminent threads; read-back to a cold reader restating each live thread.

**Kernel: triage-weighted scene log with a read-back test.**

- **Proportional detail:** detail follows thread heat, not table time. In the baseline, the long goblin scene had 9 bullets while Brandon's Tomb, where the hot threads converge, had 4.
- **Read-back fidelity:** a judge LLM reads only the Summary, restates the threads, and the result is checked against the Ledger. This is cheap to run with the existing prompt harness.

**Open assumption:** the LLM judges a thread's heat by narrative intuition, not vital signs, and may disagree with the GM. The judge adds cost and its own bias.

**User reaction:** "agreed". The agent recorded read-back as at least the scoring method; whether it is also a dimension remains open.

### 4. Memory Science: Retrieval Cues (Far)

**Sources:**

- **Encoding specificity** (Tulving and Thomson, 1973). A cue "works only to the extent that it overlaps with the information encoded alongside the target". People failed to recognize words when prompted with strong general associates, yet recalled them when given the original weak cue from the moment of learning ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encoding_specificity_principle); [Baylor cognition lab text](https://openbooks.library.baylor.edu/cognitionlab/chapter/encodingspecificity/); both via search summary).
- **Cue overload** (Watkins and Watkins, 1975). "The more memories that are associated with a specific cue, the less effective that cue will be" ([UPEI Intro Psychology](https://pressbooks.library.upei.ca/upeiintropsychology/chapter/retrieval/); [Goldsmiths, *Frontiers in Psychology*](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/21697/6/fpsyg-08-01830.pdf); both via search summary).
- **Limit:** these are largely laboratory findings with word lists. Applying them to recalling game Sessions is the agent's inference.

**Mechanism:** cues work by matching the distinctive context of the original experience. Generic or abstract restatements match many memories and retrieve none of them clearly.

**Transfer (pushes back on domains 1–3):**

- Vivid table details such as "Apprentice", the pocketed ooze or "his boss sucks" may be the most effective cues when fused into a bullet that matters, though they are noise as standalone bullets.
- Generic bullets and titles ("Upper Level") are overloaded cues.
- Abstraction can be weaker than the specific image: "made a deal with the goblins" versus "Phidipaldi took Grimble's speaking stick and claimed to be Brandon's son".

**Kernel: distinctive-hook anchors.** A **cue distinctiveness** dimension: every anchor and title carries a hook specific to this Session, in indirect speech because quotation marks remain banned. Vivid detail is allowed only fused into an anchor, never standalone.

**Open assumption:**

- The Ledger loses laughter and emphasis, so the LLM may pick the wrong hooks.
- Hooks add some words.
- Absent Players have no stored memory to cue.

**User reaction:** "agreed" to allowing fused hooks.

### 5. Harry Beck's London Underground Map (Far)

**Sources:**

- Beck "believed that Underground passengers were not concerned with geographical accuracy". He designed for how riders get "from one station to another and where to change trains" ([Wikipedia: Harry Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Beck), fetched).
- Inspired by an electrical schematic, he spaced stations "more-or-less equally" and used "only straight lines and 45 degree angles" (same source).
- The map shows "relationships rather than distances to scale". It was "considered too radical at the time", and topological metro maps became the norm ([London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/harry-beck-revolutionised-tube-map/), via search summary).

**Mechanism:** keep the structure users navigate by (connections and interchanges), discard the faithful dimension they don't need (geography and scale), and render what's left on a regular, minimal grammar.

**Transfer:**

| Tube map | Scene log |
|---|---|
| Geography | Chronology and table time |
| Lines | Live threads |
| Stations | Scenes |
| Interchanges | Moments where threads cross |
| Regular grammar | Every scene in the same terse form |

Brandon's Tomb is an interchange of the dragon, church and Alfred threads. Statues and Chapel is a station almost no line stops at.

**Kernel: thread schematic.** A **thread legibility** dimension: a reader can see which live thread each scene moved and where threads meet. The radical version organizes the log by thread rather than time, echoing the recap scholar's "reordering old scenes within new sequences".

**Open assumption:**

- Chronology may matter to players far more than geography matters to riders.
- The current Summary contract makes the scene log chronological.
- A thread-organized log could duplicate Open Loops.

**User reaction:** "yes" to bending chronology. The degree remains open: anything from thread-evident chronological bullets to a fully thread-organized log.

## Reserve Domains (Not Explored)

- **Game master prep practice (near):** clue- and secret-oriented session notes. This could validate the kernels against real table practice.
- **BLUF and military situation reports (adjacent):** fixed slots and need-to-act compression. Likely to overlap with the I-PASS findings.
- **Annotated chess games (far):** bare notation everywhere, commentary only on turning points. Likely to overlap with proportional detail.

## How the Findings Map to the Proposed Rubric

| Rubric dimension in [rubric.md](../rubric.md) | Main sources |
|---|---|
| Anchor Coverage | Case briefs (facts used in the Argument); recaps (payoff-driven selection) |
| Materiality | Case-brief deletion test; recap signal honesty |
| Proportional Detail | I-PASS severity triage |
| Cue Distinctiveness | Encoding specificity; cue overload |
| Economy | Recap clips; I-PASS compressed history |
| Thread Legibility | Tube map |
| Fact–Significance Separation | Case briefs' rule against editorializing in the facts |
| Read-back test (scoring method) | I-PASS synthesis by receiver |

The agent noted that these ideas overlap (anchor coverage, proportional detail and thread legibility all concern ties to live threads). A rubric needs independently judgeable dimensions, so overlap remains a review item.

## Open Questions Carried into Rubric Definition

- **Latent resources:** should an element with possible future utility count as material without a forward item pointing to it, or should the forward sections surface resources?
- **Read-back:** a scoring method only, or also a dimension?
- **Chronology:** how far may it bend?
- **Absent Players:** accept that hooks do not work for them as a limitation, or add a dimension for them?
- **Overlap:** should any dimensions be merged?
- **Scale format:** the project workflow's 0–10 scale with anchors, or the qualitative form used by the parent investigation?
