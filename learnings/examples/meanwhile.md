**Follow-up** (verbatim, 2026-10-23):
> 2 - I like the way you said "It does not establish an unfolding rescue problem..."   My point about no expectation or ask for help is that this is *not* an unfolding rescue problem, and was trying to find a litmus test for establishing that.

> 4 - my only push back here is that this is existing trouble more than brewing trouble.

> 6 - Right he is actually an opportunity (albiet a disturbing one) for the party, not a danger that the party has to stop

**Gill's sons, 2026-11-01** (the interview that settled the "Maybe-"):
> Yes, someone else might win a potential future goal that they have, but just failure to achieve a goal if it doesn't have narrative trouble to it beyond just the party acquiring new good stuff is not itself a trouble.

> No, that is exactly how I feel. And in addition, the adventure itself doesn't pose Gil's sons as being in danger, really. And there's, you know, no quest put forward to save them, which they either implicitly or explicitly agree to. But it is really the lack of the danger thing.

> Yes, absolutely. This is not about whether or not the players took action; it's about the way the adventure frames Gil's sons.

**Spot it:**
- Is a specific harm articulated, and is the danger established by play rather than forecast by the extractor?
- Is the harm more than someone else getting a benefit first?
- Is it developing toward something the party could affect, and external to the party?
- Does the wording predict more than the evidence supports ("starving")?

### EX-050 A threat worded so it does not overstate what is known
- Verdict: good (the user's intent; final wording agreed) · Session 002 golden · SRC-01
- Rules: [PREF-037](../preferences.md#pref-037), [PREF-031](../preferences.md#pref-031)

The user's draft said the giant "may be in the first [forest] north of town". The reviewer suggested: "The giant you robbed of Brother Dirk is awake, and his cottage is on your road north."

**User's words:**
> I understand your point. My reason for saying maybe is that I wanted a statement that highlighted the threat and danger without overstating what we know. I guess we could also say something like, the giant is angry that the party stole its food.

**Final golden** (verbatim):
> The giant is angry that you stole his dinner, Brother Dirk. His cottage is on your road north.

In the same golden, Present Danger is "- None."

**Spot it:** a threat names what is established and adds no predicted action. An empty danger section is fine.
