## One Opening per Plan

### EX-040 The steps of one opportunity listed as separate opportunities
- Verdict: bad · Session 002 Opportunities, 2026-10-24; recurrence in unified pass 12 · SRC-31, SRC-60
- Rule: [PREF-045](../preferences.md#pref-045) (patterns 2 and 3)

**The text** (verbatim openings of four separate opportunities):
> Warwick's shop holds a mithril chain shirt that is clearly magical.
> Warwick sleeps in a second-story bedroom and leaves its window cracked open.
> Ingrid the Alchemist is brewing a sleeping potion for Warwick from faun wine that Dunk diluted to about 20% strength.
> Ingrid said she could make the sleeping potion but needed wine from the Faun Grove, north of town across the river, where the fauns trade wine for sentimental items.

**User's words** (dictated: "steroids" is steps, "septate" separate, "rain wine" faun wine):
> We have three opportunities about the sleeping potion, Warwicks window, and the rain wine. These are all steroids in getting the opportunity of getting Warwicks chain mail. They are not septate opportunities.

**Pass 12 recurrence** (verbatim, two bullets):
> If the party helps Ingrid give Warwick her sleeping potion, Dunk can search his home for the mithril shirt unnoticed. The potion isn't finished, and the diluted faun wine may weaken it.
> Warwick sleeps in the second-story bedroom of his shop, and its window is left cracked open.

> I prefer merged. Why are they not in the if.... then... Format?

The user then approved this merged form (Session-specific; never copy it into a prompt): "If Dunk uses the open window and Ingrid's sleeping potion, he could search for the shirt unnoticed. The unfinished potion's strength is uncertain because he diluted the wine." — "Great, I approve"

**Spot it:** would taking this opening give an advantage toward a goal, or only make another opening possible? Do two openings each supply part of one action?

**History:** on 2026-10-18 the user asked for the window as its own line in the Session 002 golden, which lists (verbatim):
> If you get the potion from Ingrid, it will keep Warwick asleep while you steal the chain shirt.
> If you climb in Warwick's second-story bedroom window, which he leaves cracked open, you can get into the smithy to steal the chain shirt.

From 2026-10-24 the pieces are one plan.

### EX-073 Equivalent offers and stages of one plan repeat as options
- Verdict: bad duplicates · Sessions 001–002, unified pass 7 · SRC-57
- Rules: [PREF-045](../preferences.md#pref-045) (patterns 1 and 2), [PREF-041](../preferences.md#pref-041)

**The text and the user's words** (verbatim pairs):
> If the party hires Lady Hilda, she will help them fight the dragon.
> If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will join them.

> These are identical: " If the party hires Lady Hilda, she will help them fight the dragon" and "If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will join them."

> If Dunk helps Ingrid get her sleeping potion to Warwick, he could get into Warwick's house to take the shirt.
> If Dunk meets Ingrid tonight, he can collect her sleeping potion and use it to put Warwick to sleep.

> These two are the same item, repeated: "If Dunk helps Ingrid get her sleeping potion to Warwick, he could get into Warwick's house to take the shirt." and  "If Dunk meets Ingrid tonight, he can collect her sleeping potion and use it to put Warwick to sleep."

> Father William says that if the party goes to Sir Brandon's Barrow Mound, they can retrieve the sword buried with him.
> If the party claims Sir Brandon's sword, they gain what is said to be the only way to kill the dragon.

> These two are the same "Father William says that if the party goes to Sir Brandon's Barrow Mound, they can retrieve the sword buried with him." and "If the party claims Sir Brandon's sword, they gain what is said to be the only way to kill the dragon."

> If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will fight the dragon alongside them.
> If the party hires Lady Hilda, they gain a seasoned fighter in chain mail with a shield.

> These are duplicates: "If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will fight the dragon alongside them." and "If the party hires Lady Hilda, they gain a seasoned fighter in chain mail with a shield."   While one has details about her chain and mail, it is the same opportunity - if you pay her, she helps you

**Spot it:** are two sentences the same paid help, the same retrieval, or successive parts of one plan? Keep genuinely distinct resources separate (EX-066).

**Outcome:** fixed in pass 8 by reconciling equivalent openings after extraction.

### EX-053 Extra detail about an offer listed as its own opportunity
- Verdict: bad · Session 001, unified passes 4–5 · SRC-55
- Rules: [PREF-035](../preferences.md#pref-035), [PREF-028](../preferences.md#pref-028)

**The text** (verbatim, consecutive bullets):
> If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will join them on the dragon hunt.
> Lady Hilda has chain mail and a shield but says she cannot kill the dragon herself. She has named no fee.

**User's words:**
> the second lady Hilda bullet is not it's own opportunity, it is just extra detail about lady hilda

Pass 5 recurrence ("If the party agrees on a fee, Lady Hilda will join the hunt, though she says she cannot kill the dragon herself."):
> Lady hilda: the first bullet says what needs explaining about the oportunity. the thing about the fee is just extra precision about the first one, and not useful.  the bit about "though she cannot kill it herself" is not a description of the opportunity.

So "If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will join them to hunt the dragon." is the good form.

**Spot it:** does the bullet name a separate action and advantage, or only describe an earlier bullet's person or offer? A caveat about what an ally can't do does not describe the opening.

**Outcome:** fixed in pass 6: "If the party pays Lady Hilda, she will join them against the dragon."

### EX-074 A goal restatement or generic item benefit is not another opening
- Verdict: bad (map bullet, possession tautology, duplicate shirt bullets); one specific shirt benefit is wanted · Session 002, unified passes 7, 11 and 12 · SRC-57, SRC-58, SRC-59, SRC-60
- Rule: [PREF-046](../preferences.md#pref-046). Allowance: [ALW-004](../allowances.md#alw-004)

**The text** (verbatim, pass 7):
> If the party checks a marked map location on its way north through the forest, they could find the map's treasure.
> Dunk says that if the party gets Warwick's mithril chain shirt, it could help them against the dragon.
> Dunk says that if the party gets Warwick's mithril chain shirt, they will have magical armor.

**User's words:**
> This is a bad opportunity, it is not a means to an advantage, it is just saying restating the goal of finding the treasure.

> This is not an opportunity, it is an explanation of the how a chain shirt helps. It is also just a tautology, 'if they get the shirt, they have the shirt': "Dunk says that if the party gets Warwick's mithril chain shirt, they will have magical armor."

**Reinterpreted 2026-11-01** (SRC-58), after re-reading the source:
> In the case of Warwick's shirt, I think in isolation, I actually really do like and care for. You know, if they can get Warwick's mithril shirt, they'll have armor they can use when fighting the dragon, is actually really good. I think the feedback I was trying to get at was that maybe there was a second one and they were part of one of these chains that we were talking about, or it was one of the other patterns that was creating a duplicate.

So the shirt's fault was duplication, the tautology and the chain with the potion plan, not usefulness against another goal. Pass 11's "Dunk says he has a plan to get Warwick's mithril chain shirt. If he gets it, it could help against the dragon." — "I am ok with this benefit actually" (ALW-004).

**Pass 12 recurrence of the map bullet:** "The party plans to check one of the map's marked locations on the way to Brandon's Barrow Mound."
> I agree. While there is a trigger here, if the party checks other locations on the map, the benefit is just that they could solve their goal, not that they will get some kind of advantage that makes achieving the goal in the future easier. This should be removed.

**Spot it:** remove the conditional phrasing. Does anything remain except doing the goal, owning the wanted item, or saying an item is useful in general?

### EX-055 A weak link between two goals listed as an opportunity
- Verdict: bad (at most a low-value obstacle, not for the Summary) · Session 001, unified passes 4–6 · SRC-55
- Rules: [PREF-035](../preferences.md#pref-035), [PREF-040](../preferences.md#pref-040)

**The text** (verbatim): "If Dunk helps kill the dragon, Gill might give him Shamus, though Gill never agreed to that trade." Dunk had asked to trade work for the mule; Gill said the big work needed was killing the dragon. No deal was made.

**User's words:**
> The dragon mule relationship is not an opportunity. If anything, it could be tracked as an impediment to being able to purchase the mule, which is that, you know, if his sons aren't back to help with the farm, then Farmer Gill is less likely to sell his mule at a reasonable price. But this is honestly a super low value thing that should not be showing up in the summary.

> Seamus: this is not an opportunity, it describing an impediment as an opportunity.    Purchasing the mule at a fair price is not possible while gill's kids are gone and the dragon is prowling the land.

**Spot it:** was an action and its reward actually offered? Does the "opening" describe a condition that stands in the way?

**Outcome:** survived passes 5 and 6; in pass 6 the model rated `action_offered: yes` while its own basis said "no terms were agreed" ([APP-030](../approaches.md#app-030)). Hidden from pass 8 on with the mule goal.

### EX-080 A paid offer of help hidden because it didn't name the goal
- Verdict: bad (missing) in pass 10; shown from pass 11 · Sessions 001–002, unified passes 10–12 · SRC-58, SRC-60
- Rules: [PREF-040](../preferences.md#pref-040), [PREF-028](../preferences.md#pref-028), [PREF-049](../preferences.md#pref-049). Allowance: [ALW-001](../allowances.md#alw-001)

**The text** (verbatim stored fact, hidden in pass 10):
> Drop Dead Ned offers himself and his killer wasp for hire at 100 gold for the week, on condition that he gets to kill people with the wasp; he would give back 5 gold per killing he is allowed if he can do what he wants with the victims. Dr. Uriah said he would think about it.

The drop reason: "generic_benefit". Lady Hilda's offer in the same scene, which mentioned the dragon, was shown.

**User's words:**
> can you do research into the drop ded ned opportunity, which to me is the same as hilda.

**Pass 11, Session 001** (verbatim, shown):
> If the party hires Drop Dead Ned for 100 gold a week, he and his killer wasp will do any job that lets him kill. He will refund 5 gold per kill if he can do what he wants with the bodies.

Asked about its wording (the review flagged a two-sentence opening), the user: "worth fixing". This approved addressing the wording, not a particular rewrite or an absolute sentence count.

**Pass 12, Session 002** (verbatim):
> Drop Dead Ned, a murderous thief, offers to join the party for 100 gold a week if he gets to kill people. Dr. Uriah is thinking it over.

> Ooh, I don't know how I feel here. I think maybe if it's the first time we mention Ned in the summary, then it is fine to add that little aside. I actually find recording the details of the contract, like the price and potentially like his payback of 5 GP per killing, to be that's much more detail and precision that I don't think needs to be included. If this is in the development section, then I think it would be fine to include the 100 gold. If this is in the opportunity section, then I think it is not necessary to define it as part of the opportunity. It is just if they hire him, you know, then he'll fight for them.

**Spot it:** is an actual offer of help hidden only because it didn't mention the goal, while a comparable offer that did is shown? Once shown, does the opening copy contract terms instead of stating the action and benefit?

**Outcome:** a general offer-matching sentence (with a fictional example) fixed it in pass 11; the goal-less Naggeneen reward stayed hidden (EX-041).

## Wording

### EX-034 A dense Opportunity, rewritten source-first
- Verdict: bad → good · port deployment, 2026-10-28 · SRC-46
- Rule: [PREF-026](../preferences.md#pref-026)

**Before** (verbatim):
> Getting the dragon to drink enough of Dunk's faun wine could knock it out so you can drive Sir Brandon's sword through its mouth, Vivian says.

**After** (verbatim, the user's target):
> Vivian says enough of Dunk's faun wine could knock the dragon out. If that works, the party could drive Sir Brandon's sword through its mouth.

**User's words:**
> I want each bullet to be easier to understand on the first reading and easier to say aloud.

**Spot it:** is the source at the front? Is the mechanism separated from its benefit, with the hedge kept? Are three or more clauses stacked in one sentence?

**Outcome:** the plain-language work item was completed, but its own record notes that the Opportunities prompt's example still ended "…, according to Father William", contradicting the rule. A prompt's examples must obey its rules (PREF-026).
